# Credit-spread source and rule verification

## Purpose

This note answers one question: what does the supplied video prescribe, and which parts are facts, empirical claims, personal rules, contradictions, or undefined ideas? It does not choose an implementation.

The primary source is Credit Spread Investing's 18 October 2025 video, ["How I Generate Monthly Income With Credit Spreads (Full Free System)"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvmDe9DHSbA). The supplied comprehensive report is a secondary summary. I checked the video's auto-generated transcript against the cited timestamps and used current OCC, Options Industry Council, Cboe, FINRA, NIST, and research-paper sources for external claims.

## Finding

The source contains a mechanically testable credit-spread core, but it does not contain one complete reproducible Strategy. It mixes a beginner setup, discretionary technical analysis, an optional stop range, two conflicting defensive-roll instructions, worked examples, and a short self-reported trading record. The supplied report makes the system look more exact than the video is. In several places it adds rules that the presenter did not state.

The payoff mechanics are sound. A bull put spread and bear call spread have limited gain and limited loss at expiration while both legs remain intact. Maximum gain is the opening credit. Maximum loss is strike width less the credit. The Options Industry Council confirms these mechanics for [bull put spreads](https://www.optionseducation.org/strategies/all-strategies/bull-put-spread-credit-put-spread) and [bear call spreads](https://www.optionseducation.org/strategies/all-strategies/bear-call-spread-credit-call-spread). Defined risk does not mean small risk, and assignment can change the position before expiration.

The claimed trading edge is not established. The presenter shows a personal dashboard with a 94% win rate and about $43,000 profit over roughly two months, but provides no Dataset Version, complete order history, fees, fill method, rejected orders, tax lots, benchmark, or out-of-sample period ([video, 1:29:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BvmDe9DHSbA&t=5350s)). Treat it as an anecdote, not validation.

## Classification key

- **Fact**: an independently supported market mechanic.
- **Presenter rule**: a rule the presenter says that he uses or recommends. It can be tested without being accepted as true.
- **Empirical claim**: a statement about observed outcomes that needs evidence.
- **Contradiction**: two source statements cannot both define the same mechanical rule.
- **Missing definition**: the source does not say enough to reproduce the rule.

## Corrections to the supplied report

These corrections should carry forward into later decision tickets.

1. Use **around 30 DTE or 30-to-40 DTE** as the presenter's stated beginner window. Do not silently turn it into a fixed 30-to-45-DTE rule.
2. Do not equate **70%-to-80% broker POP** with **0.15-to-0.20 Delta**. They are different inputs until a precise probability event and provider calculation are named.
3. Do not call the Microsoft snapshot a typical 20%-to-25% monthly return. It is one worked quote example.
4. Do not treat **100,000 aggregate option volume** as sufficient proof of liquidity.
5. Do not add an **80%+ put-spread allocation**. The presenter only says puts should dominate.
6. Do not add a **three-to-five non-correlated ticker** rule. The presenter shows four selected tickers but states no such diversification rule.
7. The earnings rule appears to enter when earnings are about four weeks away and exit during earnings week. It does not prescribe holding through the announcement for IV crush.
8. The video says to roll at both profitable time checkpoints. The report changes one to "close or roll" and the other to "close immediately."
9. Keep the defensive-roll conflict visible. The video first permits a credit roll, then recommends closing instead.
10. Keep the position-sizing denominator unresolved. The video's large- and small-capital examples use different bases.

## Claims that survive verification

- The two-leg vertical structures, expiration payoff, maximum gain, maximum loss, and breakeven formulas are correct.
- Time decay is nonlinear. Near-expiry at-the-money options also have higher Gamma, so faster Theta does not make short-dated positions safer by itself.
- Delta can be a rough proxy for probability of expiring ITM, but it is not an exact probability and is not the same as a spread's probability of profit.
- The cited tastylive SPY study exists and reports the call/put asymmetry described in the video. Its sample does not justify a fixed put allocation or a claim about all Securities.
- American-style equity options can be exercised before expiry. Early-assignment likelihood depends on time value, moneyness, expiry, dividends, and corporate events, not only a strike crossing.
- Stop prices do not guarantee execution prices. A test that assumes each loss ends exactly at the requested stop would overstate control of the left tail.
- Earnings can cause IV to rise before an announcement and fall after it. That pattern alone does not prove that short premium earns positive returns after realized moves and costs.
